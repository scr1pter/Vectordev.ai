# Free models inside of Vector

This describes the free-model section from desktop **1.99.109** and the next CLI release, and what earlier versions do. The current downloads are desktop 1.99.108 and npm CLI 1.99.99. Whether Vector's shared allowance serves models is set on Vector's service with `FREE_MODELS_ENABLED`, not by the release; see [Shared allowance for Vector accounts](#shared-allowance-for-vector-accounts).

**The shared allowance is the free path (owner decision, 17 October 2026).** In the owner's words: "i want it so that the vector app is connected to my openrouter account no matter who uses the free models". Every signed-in Vector account uses Free models inside of Vector on Vector's shared allowance, which runs on Vector's own OpenRouter account. Nothing in the free-model flow (the composer's first-run card, Getting Started, the Free models inside of Vector card, limit and unavailable messages, the TUI and the CLI) asks anyone to connect an OpenRouter account of their own; signed out, it says "Sign in to Vector to use free models." Two routes still reach the same section:

- **Shared Vector allowance.** Any signed-in Vector account, with no OpenRouter account, while Vector's service has it on. Requests pass through vectordev.ai, which checks the account and its limits. A free model chosen while an OpenRouter account was connected runs here too once that account is gone, if the shared catalog lists it.
- **Your OpenRouter account.** OpenRouter stays an ordinary provider in Settings → Providers, not promoted anywhere else. An account connected there lists its own free models, marked Your OpenRouter account, and its requests go directly to OpenRouter with the stored key instead of the shared allowance.

Names omit the `:free` suffix; routing IDs keep it. A failed catalog check never authorizes an unlisted endpoint or a paid fallback, and `max_price` stays zero on every request.

## Every free model, with its data policy (owner decision, 17 October 2026)

The owner decided that every free OpenRouter model belongs here, including models whose providers may train on prompts, with a clear disclaimer. Each model and the provider serving it carry one of these policies, from a reviewed record in `REVIEWED_FREE_ENDPOINTS` in `packages/core/src/free-model-catalog.ts` (reviewed 17 October 2026, `FREE_MODEL_POLICY_REVIEWED`):

| Policy | Models on 17 October 2026 (provider) | What Vector does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No training | North Mini Code (Cohere, kept up to 30 days), Dots3-Note Preview (AtlasCloud, retention not published), Xing4.0 29B A4B (Phala, kept none) | Offered on both routes. Every request sends `data_collection: "deny"`. Phala serves Xing4.0 under RedPill's terms (7 April 2025), which do not address free use; the owner's decision of 17 October 2026 puts every free model on the shared allowance unless its terms bar it, so Xing4.0 moved there from the user's own account only. |
| No training, own account only | Gemma 4 26B and 31B (Google AI Studio, kept up to 55 days) | Barred from the shared allowance, so offered only through a user's own OpenRouter account, and 1.99.108 and CLI 1.99.99 never receive them. Google's Gemini API terms (28 April 2026) let EEA, Swiss and UK users use the service only through Paid Services, and the shared allowance does not check where its users are. Set in `PERSONAL_ONLY_FREE_MODELS`; serving them on the shared allowance needs that region limit applied, or a finding that it does not apply. Every request sends `data_collection: "deny"`. |
| May train | Laguna S 2.1 and XS 2.1 (Poolside, free until 31 October 2026), LFM2.5-2.6B (Liquid), Inkling and Inkling Small (Thinking Machines), and OpenRouter's Free Models Router (`openrouter/free`, see [below](#openrouters-free-models-router)) | Labelled **May train on your prompts**. Nothing is sent until the user accepts once. After that, requests send `data_collection: "allow"`, which OpenRouter needs to route to these endpoints, and fall back only to may-train models of the same provider, never to another provider or to a no-training model. |
| Unreviewed | Any free model or serving provider not in the record | Disclosed the same way as may-train, never as no-training. Offered on the personal route only, and its requests still send `data_collection: "deny"`. |
| Barred | NVIDIA's six free endpoints | Never listed. NVIDIA's API Trial Terms of Service limit them to evaluation and bar production use and sharing output (sections 1.2, 1.4 and 4.2). The reason is in `BARRED_FREE_AUTHORS`. |

No free endpoint was marked as publishing prompts on 17 October 2026. OpenRouter's public API has no training flag; the policies come from each endpoint's `data_policy` on its model page at `https://openrouter.ai/<model id>` and the terms that endpoint links, which OpenRouter calls its best knowledge rather than a definitive source. Evidence per pair is in [free-models-legal.md](owner-actions/free-models-legal.md#evidence-per-model-17-october-2026). To add a model, record its evidence there, then add its pair to `REVIEWED_FREE_ENDPOINTS`.

Still excluded: router aliases other than the Free Models Router (`openrouter/*`), models priced per item although their token prices are zero (Google Lyria, which charges per song or clip, generates music and has no tools), models without tools, and anything expiring within 7 days. Of the 19 zero-price IDs OpenRouter listed on 17 October 2026, 11 are offered (the router included, and Gemma's two only through a user's own account) and 8 are not: NVIDIA's six, barred, and Lyria's two, not free. Endpoints that accept tools but not `tool_choice` (Thinking Machines) are kept; an automatic tool choice is sent without the parameter.

The user's own OpenRouter key is held to the same list. Every model in OpenRouter's regular list that runs on free endpoints (`:free` variants, also with a variant after them such as `:free:nitro`, the free router `openrouter/free`, stealth models and any model listed at a zero price) is removed from it, on the legacy and V2 routes. The check looks at both the model's name and the API ID it sends, so a repository that configures one under another name (`provider.openrouter.models["alias"] = { id: "poolside/laguna-s-2.1:free" }`, or `api.id` on V2) is held to it too, and so is any provider, whatever it is called, whose base URL is on openrouter.ai or that uses OpenRouter's own SDK package. On V2, where repository configuration loads after the provider plugins, a last step removes such models again. One that reaches the engine anyway is sent only through the free-model checks, which refuse it: "This model runs on OpenRouter's free endpoints but isn't one of the Free models inside of Vector, so Vector doesn't send it. Nothing was sent. Choose a model from Free models inside of Vector." Sent as a regular OpenRouter model, it would skip the guest rule, the training notice and `data_collection: "deny"`. A router with no listed price, such as `openrouter/auto`, is not treated as free. A host written with a trailing dot (`openrouter.ai.`, also `openrouter.ai%2E`) is OpenRouter too.

A request that goes to OpenRouter as a regular model must also name one model, and not a free one, since OpenRouter could otherwise answer with a free model outside these checks. On both engines, a request whose configuration sets `models` (fallbacks), `route`, `preset` or `plugins` for OpenRouter (in the model's or its provider's options or request body, or in a variant), or names a preset in its model ID (`@preset/…`), is refused before anything is sent: "This model's configuration sets models for OpenRouter, which lets OpenRouter answer with another model, possibly one of its free models outside the checks for Free models inside of Vector, so Vector doesn't send it. Nothing was sent. Remove that from the model's or its provider's configuration." On the legacy engine, `model` in a model's options replaces the model the SDK sends, so one naming a free model is refused with the "isn't one of the Free models inside of Vector" message. Provider preferences (`provider`, such as `data_collection: "deny"` or `zdr`) stay allowed: they only choose among the endpoints of the model named. So OpenRouter's model fallbacks, presets and plugins, its web search plugin among them, are not available for regular OpenRouter models in Vector.

### OpenRouter's Free Models Router

`openrouter/free` is OpenRouter's Free Models Router: it is listed at a zero price and sends each request to a free model it picks at random from those that support the request's features. Vector lists it as **Free Models Router** under Free models inside of Vector, on the shared allowance and on a user's own OpenRouter account, labelled **May train on your prompts** with its own disclaimer, since the model it picks may be one whose provider trains on prompts. It goes out only after the one-time acceptance, never for a session an invited guest has worked in, and never to 1.99.108 or CLI 1.99.99 (whose catalog check would refuse it).

- A router request keeps `max_price` at zero, `require_parameters: true` and the disabled plugins, has no fallbacks (`models: ["openrouter/free"]`), and is never a fallback for another model. It cannot name providers with `only`, since the router chooses the model, so it sends `data_collection: "allow"` and `ignore: ["nvidia"]`.
- **NVIDIA is not guaranteed to be excluded.** OpenRouter's documentation for the router and for provider routing (read 17 October 2026) does not say whether a router honours `provider.ignore`, and Vector makes no live calls to find out. So the router may still pick one of NVIDIA's trial endpoints, whose terms bar this use; that is why it is offered only after the acceptance, and its disclaimer says so. Adding `nvidia` to the shared account's ignored providers is a second guard ([owner steps](owner-actions/openrouter.md#free-models-of-17-october-2026-owner-steps)).
- OpenRouter publishes no output limit for the router, so Vector caps its replies at 8,192 tokens, the smallest limit of any tool-capable free model on 17 October 2026 (LFM2.5-2.6B). Its context is OpenRouter's listed 200,000 tokens, though the model it picks may accept less.
- Vector never picks the router for you: every automatic choice (a new session's default, the first-run pick, the engine's and ACP's default per provider, the desktop's and TUI's fallback to a provider's first model) skips it, even when it is a provider's only model, and the shared catalog lists it last.

**Answers-only models.** No free model without tools is offered. On 17 October 2026 the only ones were NVIDIA's, which are barred anyway. Whether the agent loop should offer a model that cannot edit files or run commands is an open owner question.

### The label, the disclaimer and the one-time consent

- Wherever a may-train or unreviewed model is listed, it says **May train on your prompts**: the desktop picker row, its tooltip and spoken label, the composer's model chip tooltip, the TUI model list, and the `vector models` caption on stderr.
- The disclaimer: "This free model's provider may log your prompts, code and outputs and use them to train its models. Vector itself never trains on your prompts. Don't use it with private repositories, secrets or personal data." An unreviewed model says Vector has not reviewed its policy yet.
- Before the first prompt to such a model, the desktop asks once (**Accept and send** or **Don't send**), and the TUI asks when the model is chosen. The draft stays in the editor if the user declines. The question names the chosen model and says that accepting covers every model labelled **May train on your prompts**, including ones Vector has not reviewed yet, now and later, until it is withdrawn. The acceptance is recorded by the engine in `~/.local/state/vector/free-model-training-consent.json`, a fixed path under the home directory, so the desktop engine (whose XDG directories sit inside the app's data folder), the TUI and the CLI on one computer share it and read it on every request. An engine on another computer, such as `vector serve` on a server, keeps its own.
- The engine refuses to build a request to such a model without that record, so nothing is sent from any client, including `vector run`, subagents and reviews. The refusal says: "... Nothing was sent. Accept it once when Vector asks, or run "vector models --accept-training", then send again." **Withdraw** on the Free models inside of Vector card in the desktop Help panel, or `vector models --decline-training`, withdraws it.
- An invited guest (`vector invite`) runs on the owner's engine, under the owner's acceptance, which the guest never gave. So a guest is not offered models labelled **May train on your prompts** (the provider list leaves them out and the consent read says no), and the engine refuses a guest's prompt (including a subtask part that names one), compaction, worker message, model switch or free-model resume that would run on one, on the legacy and V2 routes, before anything is saved: "Only the workspace owner can use free models labelled "May train on your prompts" here, since only they can accept that notice. Choose another model." The same text replaces "try again" when an acceptance is refused with 403.
- The guest's input stays in the session's history and goes out again with every later request there, so the rule also holds where each free-model request is built (`FreeModels.resolveRoute`, used by the legacy fetch and the V2 runner alike). Once a guest has sent a session any request other than a read (GET or HEAD), or answered one of its questions, the whole session tree (its subagents and its parent) is marked in `FreeModelGuests`, and no request for it goes to a labelled model, the owner's own prompts, compactions, titles, summaries, subagents, retries and fallbacks included: "An invited guest has worked in this session, and only the workspace owner can accept the "May train on your prompts" notice, so Vector doesn't send this session to free models with that label. Nothing was sent. Choose another model." Marks are kept beside the database (`free-model-guest-sessions.txt`, one session per line, appended so two engines marking at once both keep their marks), so a restart does not clear them, and a fork of a marked session, which copies the guest's messages, is marked too. A guest request forwarded to a remote workspace reaches that workspace's engine with the owner's credentials, so it carries `x-vector-guest-request: 1`, and that engine marks the session the same way, for prompts and for answers to questions. The forwarding engine marks the session too, and warping a marked session into a remote workspace sends the same header with the steal, so the mark follows the session between engines. A session imported from an archive, or warped back from a remote engine where a guest worked in it directly, starts unmarked. The remote engine's early refusals still see an owner there, so a forwarded guest prompt to a labelled model is refused when it is sent, not before it is saved.
- Vector never chooses a may-train model for you ahead of a no-training one.
- Vector itself never trains on prompts. That is a statement about Vector, not about the providers.

### Old clients keep a no-training catalog

Desktop 1.99.108 and CLI 1.99.99 never show the label, so vectordev.ai serves may-train models only to clients that declare they show it: `GET /api/free-models/models?disclosure=may-train-v1`, and the header `x-vector-free-model-disclosure: may-train-v1` on shared chats. Any other client receives the no-training models only, and its shared chats fall back only among them. The shared chat refuses a may-train model with 403 `FREE_MODELS_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` when the disclosure is missing and 403 `FREE_MODELS_CONSENT_REQUIRED` without `x-vector-free-model-training-consent: accepted`, before any allowance is spent.

## Limits, and what Vector says at one

The shared allowance runs on Vector's OpenRouter account, which has bought $10 of credits (owner, 17 October 2026). OpenRouter sets the free tier by credits purchased over the account's life, so the account gets **1,000 free-model requests a day** instead of 50, and keeps it as the balance is spent; OpenRouter states no expiry. The limit is still **20 requests a minute**, counted across all the account's keys, so every user of the shared allowance shares both. Failed attempts count, and one coding task can use many requests, since each tool step is one. See [OpenRouter's current limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api_reference/limits).

At a limit, Vector says what happened, when the shared allowance is expected to reset, and what else to do, never connecting OpenRouter: "To keep working now, choose a model from another provider you've connected, or add your own provider key in Settings → Providers." The TUI and CLI say `/connect` or `vector providers login` instead of Settings. A user who already connected OpenRouter can still continue the same turn on it. Vector never switches to a paid model or provider. Desktop 1.99.108 and CLI 1.99.99 write their own limit text from the typed limit, which still mentions OpenRouter; only a new release changes that.

**Never let the shared account's balance go negative.** OpenRouter refuses free models with HTTP 402 on an account with a negative balance, which takes the whole shared allowance down (Vector shows it as "Shared free models are temporarily unavailable"). Every shared request sends a zero `max_price` and disables paid plugins, and every one but the Free Models Router's names only zero-price endpoints, so free use does not spend the credits; keep automatic top-ups, paid upstream BYOK keys and default or enforced paid plugins off on that account, and use it for nothing else.

A personal OpenRouter account connected in Settings → Providers has its own limits: 50 free-model requests a day without a credit purchase. Keep it free of purchased credits, payment methods, automatic top-ups, paid BYOK keys and paid plugins if it should cost nothing; those settings are outside Vector's control.

## Your OpenRouter account's privacy settings

OpenRouter has separate privacy settings for free and paid models. If a user's account does not allow free models from providers that may train on their data, OpenRouter does not list those models for that account, so they do not appear under **Your OpenRouter account**. When such a model was chosen from the shared allowance, or OpenRouter answers that no endpoint matches the data policy for a may-train model, Vector says: "Your OpenRouter privacy settings block free models whose providers may train on prompts. To use this one, open openrouter.ai/settings/privacy, allow free models from providers that may train on your data, then send again. Or choose a model without the "May train on your prompts" label." A model Vector has not reviewed is sent `data_collection: "deny"` by Vector itself, so no account setting can help; for it Vector says that it has not reviewed the model's data policy, asked OpenRouter for providers that don't train on prompts, found none, and suggests another free model.

## Start free

In the desktop app, the composer's card (shown while no connected provider offers a model) says "Sign in to Vector to use free models." and offers **Sign in to Vector** and **Use my own API key**; **Start free** in Getting Started starts the same sign-in. Once signed in, the shared models load, the card goes away and the composer selects a free model, a no-training one first; the draft prompt stays ready to send. If the shared allowance serves nothing while you are signed in, the card says Free models inside of Vector are unavailable right now and offers your own API key. The web build, which cannot sign in itself, says instead: "To use free models, sign in to Vector on the computer this page connects to: run vector login there, then restart Vector. Or use an API key you already have.", and offers the key only. In the terminal, the TUI home screen says "Sign in to Vector to use free models: run vector login." while the shared catalog is on, or that the free models are unavailable right now, and offers `/connect` for your own key. Signed out while the shared catalog is on, `vector models` prints the same line on stderr, and `vector run` adds it to its message that no model provider is connected:

```sh
vector login
vector models
vector models --accept-training   # only if you want models labelled "May train on your prompts"
```

`vector models` keeps canonical `provider/model` selectors on stdout. Its free-model heading, display names, source captions, labels and the disclaimer go to stderr.

**Your own OpenRouter account.** Settings → Providers → OpenRouter connects one through browser authorization (its callback is on the engine's localhost, so a remote engine needs an API key) or an API key; `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in the engine's environment works too. A key only in a repository's `provider.openrouter.options.apiKey` does not list its free models.

## Shared allowance for Vector accounts

`FREE_MODELS_ENABLED=false` keeps the shared allowance off without disabling a personal OpenRouter connection. A model available through an OpenRouter account the user connected uses that account's copy.

The daily refresh (03:17 UTC, `/api/free-models/refresh`) reads OpenRouter's public `/api/v1/models`, Vector's account list `/api/v1/models/user` (which applies that account's privacy settings), and, for each reviewed candidate, `/api/v1/models/{author}/{slug}/endpoints`. It keeps zero-price, tool-capable, reviewed endpoints that are online and more than 7 days from expiry, plus the Free Models Router, which lists no endpoints and is judged by its own listing, and leaves out personal-only models (`PERSONAL_ONLY_FREE_MODELS`, Gemma today), which a stored catalog also never serves. One unreadable endpoint list drops only that model; a failed model or account list keeps the stored catalog. When no model with endpoints of its own qualifies, it publishes `enabled: false`: the Free Models Router alone never keeps the section on, and a stored catalog holding only the router is served as off to every client. The catalog is stored under `vector:free-models:catalog:v4:policy` for 54 hours, so one failed nightly run does not take the section down, and every stored endpoint must carry the policy the reviewed record gives it.

`/api/free-models/models` returns a 503 whose code says why: `FREE_MODELS_CATALOG_MISSING` (never refreshed under the current key), `FREE_MODELS_CATALOG_STALE`, `FREE_MODELS_CATALOG_INVALID` or `PERSISTENT_STORE_UNAVAILABLE`.

For hosted activation, set the Vector account/token and persistent Redis configuration, `OPENROUTER_FREE_API_KEY`, `CRON_SECRET` and `FREE_MODELS_ENABLED=true` on the server, then run the refresh; see [owner actions](owner-actions/openrouter.md#free-models-of-17-october-2026-owner-steps). `FREE_MODELS_MINUTE_PER_USER` defaults to 10 (at most 20) and `FREE_MODELS_DAILY_PER_USER` to the smaller of 20 and a tenth of the account's daily free limit (at most 1,000). With the account's 1,000 a day, set `FREE_MODELS_DAILY_PER_USER=50`: enough for a few agent tasks a day, while 20 users at that rate reach the account's limit, after which everyone sees "Today's shared free model allowance is used up" until the next UTC day. These settings do not raise OpenRouter's account-wide quota, which every user of the shared allowance shares.

## Clients after an outage

A client caches the catalog in `free-models-v2.json`. When vectordev.ai answers with a refusal such as 503, the client treats the shared catalog as off instead of listing models whose prompts would fail. A network failure keeps the last catalog it served. When the catalog later turns on, the engine rebuilds its provider list without a restart.

Prompts pass through OpenRouter and the selected provider. Vector does not request zero data retention for these endpoints. Most keep prompts under their own policy; OpenRouter records Phala's Xing4.0 endpoint as keeping none. The routing rules cover the model requests only, not Vector's local history, OpenRouter account logging or other connected services.
