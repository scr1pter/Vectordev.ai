# Vector Codium

Codium is Vector's managed coding-model service: monthly subscriptions plus one-time top-ups, with the same curated model catalog at every price. The workspace stays free. Larger purchases buy more usage, not a different quality tier. **Stripe is intentionally disconnected and `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=false`; source changes do not activate sales.** The owner has deferred payment setup.

The public `/codium` pricing page (`/pricing` redirects to it) shows the offering without requiring sign-in. While sales are off, its purchase buttons say "Coming soon" and do nothing. Once enabled, a signed-in visitor's button opens the same checkout or top-up session the account page uses; a signed-out visitor signs in first, and the page resumes that purchase when they return in the same tab. The page is prerendered from `publicModelPlans()`, so changing sales flags or allowances takes a redeploy, as the API functions already do. The account page adds authenticated balances and billing management. The desktop and CLI show the provider as **Vector Codium**, while its stable internal ID remains `vector-plan`.

The page tells buyers that Codium works in the Vector desktop app and CLI, so keep `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=false` until a published desktop and CLI release includes the Vector Codium provider. Its balance answer names only the account page; when that release shows Codium balances in the app, name the app there as well.

Neither public pages nor API answers expose credit amounts. The account page, the app and the CLI show the share of this month's credits remaining, in whole percent, and whether purchased credits are available, running low or used up; the account page also shows each offer's usage as a range of typical tasks on the default model. Purchased credits pool every top-up ever bought, so a share of them would mislead a repeat buyer: right after a fresh purchase it could read 8% left. The backend accounts in provider-cost USD and keeps those amounts on the server: the authenticated status (`/api/model-plans/status`) answers the account's own monthly share, purchased-credit state and flags, and `/api/model-plans/config` answers no credit figures at all. Renewal, expiration, carry-forward and cancellation terms remain visible before purchase; `/legal/terms#codium` describes variable consumption and preserves billing-error remedies and mandatory consumer rights.

## How buying and using credits works

This is the whole path from a purchase on vectordev.ai to a reply in Vector, in plain terms.

**Buying.** Someone opens vectordev.ai/codium, signs in to their Vector account and picks a monthly plan ($10, $50, $100 or $200 a month) or a one-time top-up of one of the same amounts. Stripe takes the payment. Coming back from checkout grants nothing by itself: Stripe's signed notification tells vectordev.ai, which asks Stripe whether the money was really paid and only then adds the credits to that Vector account. Monthly credits last for the paid month and do not roll over; top-up credits carry over until they are used. While Stripe is not connected, as today, every buy button says "Coming soon".

**Getting them into Vector.** There is nothing to install, copy or paste. The desktop app and the CLI already hold the person's Vector sign-in: the app from signing in, the CLI from `vector login` (or `VECTOR_CLI_TOKEN`). With it, Vector asks vectordev.ai which Codium models the account may use, and a provider called **Vector Codium** appears in the model picker, in the app and in the terminal, next to the included free models and any keys the person connected. Its models are DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, listed first as the recommended default, Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Flash and Claude Haiku 5.5, each labelled "Codium credits". Opening the model picker reads the list again, so Codium shows up even when vectordev.ai was slow to answer at first, and signing in or out needs no restart.

**Choosing a Codium model.** Vector never picks a Codium model by itself: not for a new session, a Vectorscope review, an agent swarm, an editor connected to Vector, or a run where Codium is the only provider. Credits are spent only after the person picks a Codium model, or names one in their configuration. Once they have, the work that session starts for them stays on Codium unless they set another model for it: its title, its subagents and its compaction use the same credits, and a swarm whose chosen model is a Codium model may route its tasks to the other Codium models.

**Using it.** Each request goes from Vector to vectordev.ai (`/api/model-plans/chat`) with the account's sign-in, never straight to a model provider. vectordev.ai checks the sign-in, checks that the account's credits cover the largest the reply could be, and forwards the request with Vector's own server-side provider key, to providers that keep no data. Monthly credits are used first; purchased credits cover a request the monthly credits left cannot. The reply streams back to Vector. What the provider charged for that request comes off the balance, and that charge stays on vectordev.ai: Vector records each Codium step's tokens, and its dollar cost totals leave Codium steps out and say so. Codium spending shows as the share of credits left, in Settings, `vector codium` and the account page.

**Seeing the balance.** Settings → Providers and Settings → Usage in the app show the plan, its renewal date, how much of this month's credits is left, as a percentage, and whether the purchased credits are available, running low or used up, the same figures the account page on vectordev.ai shows. **Buy credits** opens vectordev.ai/codium and **Manage plan** opens the account page. `vector codium` prints the same in the terminal. When less than a tenth of the month's credits, or of the smallest top-up, is left, Vector says the account is running low, and when too few are left for a reply it says that. No Vector screen shows an amount of credits: how much model usage a purchase includes is a private server setting, and the account's own signed-in balance request answers a share and states, never amounts.

**Running out.** When the credits are used up, the next message stops before anything reaches a model. The session says why, for example "Your included model credits are used up. They renew at the next billing period.", and offers **Buy credits**. A request also stops when the credits left cannot cover the longest reply it could get, which is what Vector asks a model for; that answer says so ("The monthly credits left cannot cover the longest reply this request could get.") and suggests a model that uses fewer credits, a shorter conversation or a top-up. Once no Codium model's longest reply fits, Settings and `vector codium` say too few credits are left for a reply. Nothing is retried, nothing moves to another paid provider, and there are no overages or automatic top-ups. After buying, the next message simply works, because every request checks the balance.

**Other answers a turn can get.**

- Signed out, or the sign-in expired: the turn offers **Sign in to Vector**.
- Codium switched off, not fully set up, or a model taken out of the catalog: the turn asks the person to choose another model.
- "Could not run this request": the provider refused it for want of credit. That is either Vector's own provider capacity or the limit of the account's own key, which is the person's allowance, for example when a provider charged more than the gateway estimated. Vector waits and tries again, as it does for a busy provider, never asks for a purchase, and says to check the balance if it keeps failing.
- A conversation too long for the model: Vector compacts it and carries on.

**Before anyone can buy.** Connect Stripe and set the server settings under "Configuration and deferred activation" below, publish a desktop and CLI release that includes the Vector Codium provider, then set `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=true`. Until then Vector lists no Codium provider, `vector codium` says Codium is not available yet, and nothing can be charged.

## The /codium page

The page follows the layout and behaviour of a familiar coding-plan pricing page with Vector's own copy, logo and colours: its own top bar and footer, a plain black background with hairline borders, Geist for type, and Vector violet only as an accent. An **Individual / Top-ups** radiogroup switches between the four monthly plans and the four one-time packs and keeps the choice in the address (`?type=topups`), so links, reloads and the back button restore it. A top-up has one name in the server configuration and on both pages, such as "Codium 10 top-up", never the "Codium 10" of the plan at the same price; Stripe checkout shows it as "Vector Codium 10 top-up". There is no free card: the workspace itself is free to download.

The public configuration and page props contain names, purchase prices, relative tier sizes and coarse precomputed task ranges. They do not contain credit allowances, provider price ceilings, task token assumptions or private commercial settings. The displayed estimates are illustrative ranges for the launch catalog, not measured Vector completion counts or guaranteed task allotments. Recheck them when models change and validate them with approved live usage before enabling sales.

Private allowances are configured in the hosting environment through `MODEL_CREDIT_MARKUP_PERCENT`, or the explicit `MODEL_PLAN_<price>_CREDITS_USD` and `MODEL_TOPUP_<price>_CREDITS_USD` overrides. There is no published default. Missing private configuration leaves every purchase unavailable. Never put live values in this repository, public pages or test fixtures. The account status endpoint returns only the authenticated account's own balances, as shares. Monthly credits expire at the end of their paid period; purchased credits carry forward separately, with no automatic top-ups or overages.

## Coding catalog

Codium offers low-cost coding models only, chosen for the most usage per credit without giving up quality. The curated allowlist lives in `api/_lib/codium-catalog.ts`; choosing Codium always requires an explicit model selection. These routing ceilings were checked against OpenRouter's public [model catalog](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models) and [ZDR endpoint catalog](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr) on October 10, 2026. They require working endpoints with function tools, no training/retention routing, and no additional request/image charges.

| Model                                                                     | Role                                    | Context / max output | Input / output ceiling per million tokens |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| [DeepSeek V4.1 Flash](https://openrouter.ai/deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash) | Default everyday model                  | 262,144 / 32,000     | $0.30 / $1.20                             |
| [Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Flash](https://openrouter.ai/xiaomi/mimo-v2.6-flash)    | Cheapest option for routine work        | 262,144 / 16,384     | $0.26 / $0.41                             |
| [Claude Haiku 5.5](https://openrouter.ai/anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5)      | Strongest low-cost model for hard tasks | 64,000 / 16,000      | $0.22 / $0.55                             |

Why these three, from independent boards that still separate low-cost models (Vals Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 2.1, Vibe Code Bench v1.1, Code Migration) rather than saturated vendor SWE-bench figures, and from cached-input price, which dominates Vector's agent sessions:

- **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash** has the best independent quality per dollar of the low-cost models (Vibe Code 84.7%, Code Migration 45.6%, Terminal-Bench 2.1 74.5%), runs at about 217 tokens/s and has the widest ZDR coverage. The $0.30 / $1.20 ceiling covers the mainstream fp8 ZDR endpoints and leaves out Fireworks US and BaseTen fast. The catalog uses 262K of its context, enough for real work; recall across the 1M window is unproven.
- **Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Flash** is the cheapest per task among credible agentic models (Terminal-Bench 2.1 76.4% at about $0.02 a task) with cached input at $0.002 to $0.004 per million on four of its five ZDR vendors. It streams at about 62 tokens/s, so output is capped at 16,384 tokens to finish inside the gateway's 600-second limit even on a slow provider. The Xiaomi first-party endpoint is not ZDR.
- **Claude Haiku 5.5** is the harder-task option. Every Haiku endpoint bills a whole request at five times the price once its prompt reaches 100,000 tokens, so its Codium route keeps prompts under that threshold. The gateway refuses any prompt its conservative count puts at 100,000 tokens or more (see below), so the catalog lists a 64,000-token window with a 16,000-token reply: Vector compacts a conversation once a step reaches 48,000 tokens, which at Vector's four characters a token stays under what that count admits for ASCII text. The gateway has no Anthropic tokenizer, so this count is a bound for code, prose, JSON and tool output, not a guarantee for every possible text: a prompt made mostly of something as dense as long random hashes could still cross the threshold.

The gateway pins DeepSeek to high reasoning, leaves MiMo at its provider default, and pins Haiku to high reasoning; a client's own reasoning setting is ignored. DeepSeek and MiMo route only to fp8/bf16 endpoints, never fp4 or endpoints of unknown precision. Haiku's endpoints have no implicit caching, so without breakpoints every input token would bill at ten times the cached price: the gateway marks explicit five-minute breakpoints at the end of the system prompt, on the latest user message and at the end of the conversation, plus the end of the first system message when the system prompt comes as several (Vector sends its stable tools and instructions first, so a new session reads them from the cache). It drops a client's own markers, on message parts and on tools alike; a tool keeps only its name, description, parameters and `strict` flag. Explicit per-block breakpoints work on every Anthropic-compatible provider. A forced `tool_choice`, `required` or one named tool, becomes `auto` for Haiku, because a forced tool call skips its reasoning, which the gateway pins on. An assistant turn keeps its earlier reasoning: Vector's clients send it as `reasoning_content`, which the gateway forwards as OpenRouter's `reasoning`, together with any `reasoning_details`, because DeepSeek's thinking mode refuses a tool-call turn whose reasoning is missing. Vector does not yet keep the signed `reasoning_details` that Anthropic models return; a live multi-step tool turn on Haiku, which needs the owner's approval, shows whether it must. Requests set `require_parameters`, so OpenRouter routes only to endpoints that accept every option sent; Haiku's endpoints take no sampling options (`temperature`, `top_p`, `seed`, penalties), so the gateway forwards only `stop`, `tools`, `tool_choice` and `response_format` for it, and no model gets `parallel_tool_calls`, which no reviewed endpoint accepts. `bun scripts/model-plan-catalog.ts` leaves out an endpoint that lacks an option the gateway may forward to that model: the model's own list, or for DeepSeek and MiMo every default option, because a request that sets one is never routed there. See OpenRouter's [prompt caching](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/prompt-caching) and [provider routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection) documentation.

General context checks use an estimated token count including tools and structured-output schemas; a provider context refusal becomes a structured Codium context error so Vector can compact. The estimate is not an exact tokenizer bound. Haiku additionally has a prompt bound: ASCII counts one token per two characters, which assumes the text is no denser than code, prose, JSON and tool output; every other character counts one token per UTF-8 byte, the most a byte-level tokenizer can produce; a JSON escape counts as the one character it stands for; and 2,000 tokens cover the framing a provider adds. The gateway refuses a Haiku request whose bound reaches 100,000 with `MODEL_PLAN_CONTEXT`, so Vector compacts instead. For ASCII text the bound never refuses a full-reply request the window check accepts; text with many non-ASCII characters, such as CJK, reaches it at about a third of the window. The other models do not inherit that restriction.

These roles are product recommendations from public benchmarks, not measured Vector results. Evaluate completion quality and latency on real Vector coding tasks before launch. Models are selected by the user; this change does not introduce automatic model routing by task difficulty. All models consume the same credit pools, and more expensive models spend them faster. Input, output and billed reasoning count toward usage. Caching can lower actual usage cost.

Do not anchor permanent prices to temporary provider discounts. From `packages/web`, `bun scripts/model-plan-catalog.ts` checks whether the configured catalog still has compatible live endpoints. A model's cheapest advertised provider may have incompatible context, tool support or retention settings. The audit ignores per-search web fees, because the gateway turns off OpenRouter's web plugin and accepts only function tools. Price ceilings fail closed if suitable provider prices rise; review the catalog rather than silently increasing customer cost.

## Account and credit flow

Supabase authenticates the web account. Stripe adapters are implemented but disconnected. A checkout redirect alone never grants credits. The gateway verifies signed Vector account tokens and durable account revocation; server-held encrypted OpenRouter keys make the actual inference requests. Neither management credentials nor inference keys reach the desktop/browser.

Monthly access requires a full paid invoice matching one supported subscription and its current period, with an unrefunded, undisputed charge. Trial, unpaid, paused, refunded, discounted, prorated-only and expired periods do not grant monthly credits. Monthly card checkout is supported initially; coupons, customer-balance-only payments, manual settlement and mid-cycle plan changes are not. The billing portal permits payment-method updates, invoices and cancellation at period end. Cancellation retains the paid remainder of the current period.

Top-up checkout uses one-time payment mode. Credit grants must be tied to an actual paid checkout and its account, price and charge, with durable purchase identity to prevent duplicate grants. Refunds and disputes remove the associated allowance. Purchased credits remain separate from monthly renewals. OpenRouter's capped keys and usage totals are authoritative for spend; a lost or repeated request cannot reset the allowance.

Before inference, the gateway conservatively checks whether the selected pool can cover the bounded request. Monthly credits are used first; purchased credits can cover a request that does not fit the monthly remainder. An upstream response never triggers an automatic retry against the other pool. A key's usage only grows once a request finishes, so each request also holds its largest possible cost against its pool in Valkey, in the same atomic step that checks the balance less what other requests hold. A request the provider refused releases its hold at once; a finished one keeps only what the provider charged, for 30 seconds, until the key's usage shows it. An answer that broke off, or that the person stopped, may still be billed when the provider finishes it, so its largest cost stays held until the hold lapses, 11 minutes after the request started and past the gateway's 600-second request limit; the same lapse frees a hold whose request never settled at all. Parallel requests therefore cannot together pass a balance that covers only some of them: one that does not fit is refused with its own message, or covered by purchased credits. OpenRouter's per-key limit stays the backstop. Vector does not charge customers automatic overages. Confirm provider behavior under load before enabling sales, and account for possible provider-side overshoot, such as a provider billing more than the requested reply, as an operating risk.

Purchased-credit reconciliation reads current payment and dispute state, with Stripe reads limited to batches of eight. This fails closed when payment verification is unavailable and prevents reordered notifications from restoring refunded credits. Verification work still grows with lifetime purchase history; test realistic account histories and provider rate limits before launch. A future durable reconciliation ledger must preserve refund, dispute and deletion correctness rather than trusting cached grants indefinitely.

Account deletion closes new purchases and model access durably, expires pending checkout, cancels recurring subscriptions and disables model keys before identity deletion. Cleanup failures remain retryable. Stripe notifications verify signatures and reconcile current payment state; duplicate or reordered notifications must not mint credits.

## Using credits in Vector

The desktop app and the CLI list **Vector Codium** (`vector-plan`) as a provider whenever the signed-in account's `/api/model-plans/models` answers `enabled: true`, which needs `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=true` and a complete server configuration. Every signed-in account then sees the catalog, with or without credits; the endpoint reads no billing state, so frequent catalog polls never reach Stripe or OpenRouter. The desktop app passes the account token from its main process to the local engine, and the CLI uses the `vct_` token from `vector login` or `VECTOR_CLI_TOKEN`. Neither the token nor project configuration can change the provider's destination or models: requests go only to `/api/model-plans/chat`, with the token added at that final boundary. A catalog refresh that is slow, fails or returns something unusable keeps the account's last catalog, so a paid turn never fails on it; only a refused token or `enabled: false` removes Codium. A fresh engine starts from the last catalog it saved for the signed-in account (keyed by a digest of the token, never the token), and a turn on an account with no catalog at all waits for vectordev.ai's answer instead of giving up after the short listing deadline.

Both runtimes (the engine's AI SDK path and the Core V2 runner) stream text and tool calls through the gateway. The gateway forwards each reply's token usage without the provider's USD charge, and the catalog Vector receives carries no prices, because dollars spent beside the share of a plan used would reveal the plan's allowance. A Codium step therefore records its tokens and counts as unpriced: session, Settings → Usage and CLI cost totals leave it out and note that some steps are not in the total. Codium is never chosen automatically, in the engine, the Core V2 runner, the app, the TUI, ACP editor sessions, Vectorscope reviews or agent swarms, even when it is the only provider listed, so credits are spent only on a model the person chose. The catalog's order is kept, and its first model is Codium's default within the provider. The app and the TUI read the provider list again whenever a model picker opens, so a catalog that arrived after the first listing is shown.

The gateway estimates a request's input tokens at four bytes of content per token, as Vector's clients do, and refuses one that cannot fit the model's listed context window with its reply (`MODEL_PLAN_CONTEXT`). Vector compacts a conversation once a step's provider-counted tokens reach the window less the reply, which comes first for text averaging up to four characters a token; for text with more characters a token the gateway's estimate reaches the window first, and its refusal makes Vector compact. Haiku requests also meet the prompt bound described under Coding catalog. A provider tokenizer can count more tokens than that estimate, so when the provider itself refuses a request's context (or answers 413), the gateway answers `MODEL_PLAN_CONTEXT` too, without passing on the provider's wording, and Vector compacts instead of retrying. The same estimate sizes the credit check that picks the monthly or purchased pool.

Settings → Providers and Settings → Usage show the plan, the share of the month's credits left, whether purchased credits are available, running low or used up, and the renewal date from `/api/model-plans/status`, which accepts the same account token as chat requests (and the website's browser session). Like the account page, they show a share and states, never amounts: vectordev.ai works out the monthly share, rounded to whole percent, the purchased credits' state and both flags, so no amount reaches the app, the CLI or the browser. Purchased credits read as a state because they pool every top-up ever bought, so a share of them would mislead a repeat buyer; they are used up once they cannot pay for the cheapest full reply, and running low under a tenth of the smallest top-up. **Buy credits** opens `/codium`; **Manage plan** opens `/account`. A balance under a tenth of the month's plan credits, or of the smallest top-up, is flagged as running low; both thresholds scale with what was bought, so the share at which the warning appears reveals no allowance, and purchased credits count every top-up ever bought, so they do not raise the threshold. A balance too small for a full reply on any Codium model, which the gateway would refuse because Vector asks each model for its longest reply, is flagged as used up. When that happens, including to a subscriber's monthly credits before they renew, Settings and `vector codium` say so and give the renewal date.

When the gateway answers that the account has no plan or not enough credits (402 with `MODEL_PLAN_REQUIRED`, `MODEL_PLAN_PAYMENT_REQUIRED`, `MODEL_PLAN_EXHAUSTED` or `MODEL_TOPUP_EXHAUSTED`), the turn fails at once with the gateway's explanation and a **Buy credits** action. A revoked or expired sign-in, or no sign-in at all, offers **Sign in to Vector**, and a disabled or misconfigured service, or a model no longer in the catalog, asks the person to choose another model. None of these answers is retried or sent to another provider. A failure Vector detects before sending (signed out, Codium off, model retired) reads exactly like the gateway's answer and sends nothing. `MODEL_PLAN_CAPACITY` (402) means the provider refused the request for want of credit: Vector's model capacity, or the account key's own limit, which is the person's allowance. It is retried like a busy provider and never asks for a purchase. Engine sessions show the action on the failed turn and on session notices; Core V2 sessions record it in the assistant error as `{ type: "codium", action }`. `vector codium` (alias `vector credits`) prints the plan and balances, and the purchase link only while Codium is on sale (signed out, it reads that from the public `/api/model-plans/config`); `vector models vector-plan` lists the models.

## Configuration and deferred activation

Server secrets belong in the hosting environment, never in client builds or the repository. See `.env.example` for the complete list:

- `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=false` while payment setup is deferred.
- `MODEL_CREDIT_MARKUP_PERCENT` sets the purchase-to-credit conversion. Set it privately; there is no fallback. If blank, explicit private allowance overrides are required before purchases can become available.
- `MODEL_PLAN_<price>_CREDITS_USD` and `MODEL_TOPUP_<price>_CREDITS_USD` set private allowances; valid values are greater than zero and no greater than the purchase price.
- `MODEL_PLAN_MODELS_JSON` optionally overrides the catalog. Blank uses the reviewed defaults; `[]` intentionally offers no models.
- `STRIPE_MODEL_PLAN_<price>_PRICE_ID` and `STRIPE_MODEL_TOPUP_<price>_PRICE_ID` map each of the four prices ($10, $50, $100 and $200) to recurring and one-time prices respectively.
- Stripe secret, webhook secret and portal configuration; OpenRouter management key; a stable 32-byte AES encryption secret encoded as 64 hex characters; existing Vector/Supabase authentication and persistent Redis/Valkey REST configuration.

`bun scripts/setup-model-plans.ts` is a **dry run by default** and shows prices/allowances without connecting to Stripe. Its explicit `--apply` adapter can later create/reuse products and the cancellation portal in the intended test account. Do not run `--apply` until the owner resumes Stripe setup. Register `/api/model-plans/webhook` using API version `2025-02-24.acacia`, with checkout, payment, subscription, invoice, charge/refund and dispute events. Keep sales disabled until the full test flow is verified.

OpenRouter's [terms, section 7](https://openrouter.ai/terms) restrict resale of model API access. Confirm that the bundled Vector offering is covered by appropriate commercial terms before activation. No source change grants this permission.

Remaining launch work requires owner authentication/business verification, funded capacity, actual payment/provider fee terms, secret configuration, legal review of the Codium terms, payment activation, and end-to-end test payments, renewal, cancellation, refund and coding-task checks. Publishing the coming-soon website does not enable paid access. No live Stripe connection, real purchase or provider funding was performed as part of this implementation.
