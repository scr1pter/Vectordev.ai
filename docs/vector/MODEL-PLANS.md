# Vector Codium

Codium is Vector's managed coding-model service: monthly subscriptions plus one-time top-ups, with the same curated model catalog at every price. The workspace stays free. Larger purchases buy more usage, not a different quality tier. **Stripe is intentionally disconnected and `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=false`; source changes do not activate sales.** The owner has deferred payment setup.

The public `/codium` pricing page (`/pricing` redirects to it) shows the offering without requiring sign-in. While sales are off, its purchase buttons say "Coming soon" and do nothing. Once enabled, a signed-in visitor's button opens the same checkout or top-up session the account page uses; a signed-out visitor signs in first, and the page resumes that purchase when they return in the same tab. The page is prerendered from `publicModelPlans()`, so changing sales flags or credit amounts takes a redeploy, as the API functions already do. The account page adds authenticated balances and billing management. The desktop and CLI show the provider as **Vector Codium**, while its stable internal ID remains `vector-plan`.

The page tells buyers that Codium works in the Vector desktop app and CLI, so keep `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=false` until a published desktop and CLI release includes the Vector Codium provider. Its balance answer and the plan cards name Vector's Settings as well as the account page. That holds once that release is published, because a balance needs a purchase and a purchase needs `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=true`: the desktop app shows Codium balances from 1.99.108.

Neither public pages nor API answers expose credit amounts. The account page, the app and the CLI show the share of this month's credits remaining, in whole percent, and whether purchased credits are available, running low or used up; the account page also shows each offer's usage as a range of typical tasks on the default model. Purchased credits pool every top-up ever bought, so a share of them would mislead a repeat buyer: right after a fresh purchase it could read 8% left. The backend accounts in provider-cost USD and keeps those amounts on the server: the authenticated status (`/api/model-plans/status`) answers the account's own monthly share, purchased-credit state and flags, and `/api/model-plans/config` answers no credit figures at all. Renewal, expiration, carry-forward and cancellation terms remain visible before purchase; `/legal/terms#codium` describes variable consumption and preserves billing-error remedies and mandatory consumer rights.

## How buying and using credits works

This is the whole path from a purchase on vectordev.ai to a reply in Vector, in plain terms.

**Buying.** Someone opens vectordev.ai/codium, signs in to their Vector account and picks a monthly plan (Plus, Pro, Max or Ultra, at $10, $50, $100 or $200 a month) or a one-time top-up of one of the same amounts. Stripe takes the payment. Coming back from checkout grants nothing by itself: Stripe's signed notification tells vectordev.ai, which asks Stripe whether the money was really paid and only then adds the credits to that Vector account. Monthly credits last for the paid month and do not roll over; top-up credits carry over until they are used. While Stripe is not connected, as today, every buy button says "Coming soon".

**Getting them into Vector.** There is nothing to install, copy or paste. The desktop app and the CLI already hold the person's Vector sign-in: the app from signing in, the CLI from `vector login` (or `VECTOR_CLI_TOKEN`). With it, Vector asks vectordev.ai which Codium models the account may use, and a provider called **Vector Codium** appears in the model picker, in the app and in the terminal, next to the included free models and any keys the person connected. Its models are DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017, listed first as the recommended default, Claude Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna, Kimi K3.1 Pro and Gemini 4 Argon, each labelled "Codium credits". Opening the model picker reads the list again, so Codium shows up even when vectordev.ai was slow to answer at first, and signing in or out needs no restart.

**Choosing a Codium model.** Vector never picks a Codium model by itself: not for a new session, a Vectorscope review, an agent swarm, an editor connected to Vector, or a run where Codium is the only provider. Credits are spent only after the person picks a Codium model, or names one in their configuration. Once they have, the work that session starts for them stays on Codium unless they set another model for it: its title, its subagents and its compaction use the same credits, and a swarm whose chosen model is a Codium model may route its tasks to the other Codium models.

**Using it.** Each request goes from Vector to vectordev.ai (`/api/model-plans/chat`) with the account's sign-in, never straight to a model provider. vectordev.ai checks the sign-in, checks that the account's credits cover the model's largest request (its whole context and its longest reply, at the catalog prices), and forwards the request with Vector's own server-side provider key, to endpoints listed as zero-data-retention. Monthly credits are used first; purchased credits cover a request the monthly credits left cannot. The reply streams back to Vector. What the provider charged for that request comes off the balance, and that charge stays on vectordev.ai: Vector records each Codium step's tokens, and its dollar cost totals leave Codium steps out and say so. Codium spending shows as the share of credits left, in Settings, `vector codium` and the account page.

**Seeing the balance.** Settings → Account and Settings → Usage in the app show the plan, its renewal date, how much of this month's credits is left, as a percentage, and whether the purchased credits are available, running low or used up, the same figures the account page on vectordev.ai shows. From desktop 1.99.109 billing happens in the app: **Buy credits** lists the plans and top-ups by name and price (never their credits) and opens Stripe Checkout in a Vector window, **Change plan** and **Cancel plan** open Stripe's customer portal at that step, and **Manage billing and invoices** (Settings → Account and Settings → Usage) opens the portal's home for the card, invoices and email. While Codium is not on sale every purchase button reads Coming soon, and while the account has also never paid for Codium, Settings → Account says Coming soon. The web build and older desktops open vectordev.ai/codium and the account page instead. `vector codium` prints the same in the terminal. When less than a tenth of the month's credits, or of the smallest top-up, is left, Vector says the account is running low, and when too few are left for a reply it says that. No Vector screen shows an amount of credits: how much model usage a purchase includes is a private server setting, and the account's own signed-in balance request answers a share and states, never amounts.

**Running out.** A request stops before anything reaches a model when the credits available cannot cover the model's largest request: when they are used up, when too few are left, or when requests still running hold them. Every one of these gets the same answer per pool, "Your monthly Codium credits cannot cover this model's largest request right now.", or the same words for purchased credits, which suggests waiting for running requests, a model that uses fewer credits, or a top-up, and the session offers **Buy credits**. The amount checked is fixed per model and never depends on the request, so a shorter conversation does not help and the answer never shows how much is left. Once no Codium model's largest request fits, Settings and `vector codium` say too few credits are left for a reply. Nothing is retried, nothing moves to another paid provider, and there are no overages or automatic top-ups. After buying, the next message simply works, because every request checks the balance.

**Other answers a turn can get.**

- Signed out, or the sign-in expired: the turn offers **Sign in to Vector**.
- Codium switched off, not fully set up, or a model taken out of the catalog: the turn asks the person to choose another model.
- "Could not run this request": the provider refused it for want of credit. That is either Vector's own provider capacity or the limit of the account's own key, which is the person's credit amount, for example when a provider charged more than the gateway estimated. Vector waits and tries again, as it does for a busy provider, never asks for a purchase, and says to check the balance if it keeps failing.
- A conversation too long for the model: Vector compacts it and carries on.

**Before anyone can buy.** Connect Stripe and set the server settings under "Configuration and deferred activation" below, publish a desktop and CLI release that includes the Vector Codium provider, then set `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=true`. Until then Vector lists no Codium provider, `vector codium` says Codium is not available yet, and nothing can be charged.

## The /codium page

The page follows the layout, type scale and behaviour of a familiar coding-plan pricing page with Vector's own copy, logo and plans. Its neutrals are the reference's warm near-blacks with a faint violet hue (page `#121118`, cards `#19181e`), Vector violet is the only accent (the "Most popular" badge), Geist is the typeface, and the logo is drawn in the text colour. The theme follows the system until a visitor picks System, Light or Dark in the footer. Under the title, an **Individual / Top-ups** radiogroup keeps the choice in the address (`?type=topups`), so links, reloads and the back button restore it.

- **Individual** shows four cards, Plus ($10), Pro ($50), Max ($100) and Ultra ($200), with Pro marked "Most popular". The names live in `packages/schema/src/codium-plan.ts`, which the server configuration, the website and the app all read; the plan ids (`vector-10` to `vector-200`) never change, so running subscriptions, Stripe metadata and links keep working. Every card states its typical tasks a month for each model. Pro to Ultra state their usage as a multiple of Plus ("5x more usage than Plus"). Pro lists what every plan includes and Max what the free workspace includes, each under a label that says so, which keeps the card heights of the reference layout. Every card carries the renewal terms.
- **Top-ups** shows two cards: a **Top-up** card whose picker chooses $10, $50, $100 or $200, and a **Monthly plans** card whose **Compare plans** button returns to Individual. A top-up is not a plan, so it is named by its amount, such as "$10 credit top-up", in the server configuration, on both pages and in the app, never by a plan's name. The page shows it in the top-up card's footnote and as each amount's accessible name, and in the button once sales open. Stripe checkout shows the name of the Stripe product, which `scripts/setup-model-plans.ts` creates as "Vector Codium $10 credit top-up".
- Below the cards: a note on taxes, renewal and carry-forward with a link to the Codium terms, a **Compare features across plans** table from 660px wide (its header row is sticky; below 1140px the table scrolls sideways, and there, as in the reference layout, the header row sits one header height lower and covers the Usage row, whose multiples the cards also state), a nine-question **Questions & Answers** list, a **Get started with Vector.** call to action and the footer.

There is no free card: the workspace itself is free to download.

The public configuration and page props contain names, purchase prices, relative tier sizes and coarse precomputed task ranges. They do not contain credit amounts, provider price ceilings, task token assumptions or private commercial settings. The displayed estimates are illustrative ranges for the launch catalog, not measured Vector completion counts or guaranteed task allotments. Recheck them when models change and validate them with approved live usage before enabling sales.

Private credit amounts are configured in the hosting environment through `MODEL_CREDIT_MARKUP_PERCENT`, or the explicit `MODEL_PLAN_<price>_CREDITS_USD` and `MODEL_TOPUP_<price>_CREDITS_USD` overrides. There is no published default. Missing private configuration leaves every purchase unavailable. Never put live values in this repository, public pages or test fixtures. The account status endpoint returns only the authenticated account's own balances, as shares. Monthly credits expire at the end of their paid period; purchased credits carry forward separately, with no automatic top-ups or overages.

## Coding catalog

The reviewed catalog in `api/_lib/codium-catalog.ts` lists five models in the order below. Selecting Codium requires an explicit model choice. DeepSeek is listed first as the default. Kimi and Gemini are deliberate premium options and use credits faster; every plan includes them. MiMo is removed from Codium.

The [public model catalog](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models) and [ZDR endpoint catalog](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr) were checked on October 23, 2026. Selected records are captured in `packages/web/test/fixtures/codium-endpoints-2026-10-23.json`; the audit tests them against the real routing configuration. `bun scripts/model-plan-catalog.ts` from `packages/web` repeats the audit against current public metadata without sending a model request.

| Model                                                                               | Role                               | Context / max output | Input / output ceiling per million tokens |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| [DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017](https://openrouter.ai/deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017) | Default everyday coding            | 262,144 / 16,384     | $0.30 / $1.20                             |
| [Claude Haiku 5.5](https://openrouter.ai/anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5)                | Focused coding and tests           | 64,000 / 16,000      | $0.22 / $0.55                             |
| [GPT-6 Luna](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-6-luna)                               | Efficient reasoning and agent work | 128,000 / 8,192      | $0.1375 / $0.55                           |
| [Kimi K3.1 Pro](https://openrouter.ai/moonshotai/kimi-k3.1-pro)                     | Premium coding and reasoning       | 128,000 / 8,192      | $2.00 / $10.00                            |
| [Gemini 4 Argon](https://openrouter.ai/google/gemini-4-argon)                       | Premium coding and analysis        | 128,000 / 8,192      | $2.00 / $10.00                            |

Routing requires `zdr: true`, `data_collection: "deny"` and `require_parameters: true` for every model. DeepSeek stays restricted to fp8/bf16. The owner approved Kimi's vendor-native mxfp4 and the closed models' unspecified native precision. There is no fallback to a non-ZDR route. The $2/$10 ceilings exclude Kimi's highspeed route and Gemini's more expensive regional/priority routes. If the standard route stops meeting these ceilings, the request fails rather than raising the price.

All five use high reasoning, pinned by the gateway. Each model forwards only reviewed options: Haiku accepts stop, tools, tool choice and response format; GPT accepts seed, tools, tool choice and response format; Kimi accepts stop, frequency/presence penalties, response format and tools; Gemini accepts temperature, top-p, seed, response format, tools and stop. DeepSeek uses the common option list. GPT uses `max_completion_tokens`; the others use `max_tokens`. Client overrides cannot change these policies.

The gateway accepts text and local function tools only and rejects media and paid add-ons before forwarding. The audit ignores image/audio/search rates this request shape cannot incur, checks reasoning prices against the output ceiling, and rejects unknown positive fees. An image-price filter is not sent because it would exclude Gemini even for a text request.

[Prompt caching](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/prompt-caching) is model-specific. Haiku and Gemini receive server-created `cache_control` breakpoints. GPT receives native `prompt_cache_breakpoint` markers with explicit 30-minute caching; its ceiling includes the Azure cache-write rate. DeepSeek and Kimi use provider caching. Client cache markers and settings are stripped. Haiku's forced tool choices become auto while reasoning is enabled. Kimi's unsupported named-tool choices become auto; Gemini keeps its supported tool choices. No cache hit is promised.

Haiku retains a conservative prompt bound below its 100,000-token price threshold. GPT uses a 272,000-token bound and a smaller working context to stay below its higher-price tier. Reply caps include reasoning tokens and are reduced for long-running models, with the existing 600-second upstream deadline retained. Public endpoints returned no recent throughput measurement during review, so no completion-time guarantee or live-performance claim is made. No paid model requests were used for this update.

The credit reservation uses the full configured context at the input ceiling plus the maximum reply at the output ceiling. Each premium model reserves $0.33792 of internal provider-cost credit until the request settles; actual provider charges settle the request. This is server accounting, not a purchase promise or public credit balance. Public $10-tier task ranges are coarse illustrations: DeepSeek 50–500, Haiku 30–300, GPT 60–600, Kimi 5–50, Gemini 5–50. Other plan ranges scale by plan price. These are not measured completion counts and do not reveal private credit settings.

The ordinary provider mirror also includes these five models. Rebuild its reviewed entries with `bun scripts/refresh-codium-mirror.ts` from `packages/web`; it reads the captured public metadata and passes the result through the release-catalog normalizer. Codium remains an authenticated dynamic catalog; the mirror does not activate paid access.

## Account and credit flow

Supabase authenticates the web account. Stripe adapters are implemented but disconnected. A checkout redirect alone never grants credits. The gateway verifies signed Vector account tokens and durable account revocation; server-held encrypted OpenRouter keys make the actual inference requests. Neither management credentials nor inference keys reach the desktop/browser.

Monthly access requires a full paid invoice matching one supported subscription and its current period, with an unrefunded, undisputed charge. Trial, unpaid, paused, refunded, discounted, prorated-only and expired periods do not grant monthly credits. Monthly card checkout is supported initially; coupons, customer-balance-only payments and manual settlement are not. The billing portal permits payment-method updates, invoices and cancellation at period end. Cancellation retains the paid remainder of the current period. While Codium is on sale and funded, a second portal configuration (`STRIPE_MODEL_PLAN_CHANGE_PORTAL_CONFIG_ID`) also changes plans: a bigger plan starts a new month today, charged in full with no proration (billing cycle anchor reset), and a smaller one is scheduled for the renewal date. Each of those is a full paid invoice for its period, so the rule above grants it; a subscription's recorded credits count only while it is still on the plan they were sold for.

The desktop app opens checkout and the portal in a sandboxed window with its own session, authenticated by the signed Vector account token (`vct_`) its main process holds. Checkout and top-up sessions accept that token as they accept the website's session. The portal, which can charge the saved card for a bigger plan and change the billing email, accepts only a token from the desktop's own sign-in, which `/api/account/cli-exchange` marks (`client: "desktop"`); a token pasted into `vector login --token`, or a desktop sign-in from before 1.99.109, gets `DESKTOP_SIGN_IN_REQUIRED`, and the desktop then opens the account page on the website. Account deletion never accepts any of these tokens. Only the window's page is held to Stripe's hosts; its frames are not, because a bank's 3D Secure check, Link and captchas load in frames on other hosts. One billing window is open at a time.

Top-up checkout uses one-time payment mode. Credit grants must be tied to an actual paid checkout and its account, price and charge, with durable purchase identity to prevent duplicate grants. Refunds and disputes remove the associated credit amount. Purchased credits remain separate from monthly renewals. OpenRouter's capped keys and usage totals are authoritative for spend; a lost or repeated request cannot reset the credit amount.

Before inference, the gateway checks whether the selected pool can cover the model's largest request, its context length at the input price plus its longest reply at the output price, a fixed amount per model that never depends on the request. Monthly credits are used first; purchased credits can cover a request that does not fit the monthly remainder. An upstream response never triggers an automatic retry against the other pool. A key's usage only grows once a request finishes, so each request also holds that amount against its pool in Valkey, in the same atomic step that checks the balance less what other requests hold. A request the provider refused releases its hold at once; a finished one keeps only what the provider charged, for 30 seconds, until the key's usage shows it. An answer that broke off, or that the person stopped, may still be billed when the provider finishes it, so its hold stays until it lapses, 11 minutes after the request started and past the gateway's 600-second request limit; the same lapse frees a hold whose request never settled at all. Parallel requests therefore cannot together pass a balance that covers only some of them: one that does not fit is refused with the same answer as any other shortfall, or covered by purchased credits. OpenRouter's per-key limit stays the backstop. Vector does not charge customers automatic overages. Confirm provider behavior under load before enabling sales, and account for possible provider-side overshoot, such as a provider billing more than the requested reply, as an operating risk.

Purchased-credit reconciliation reads current payment and dispute state, with Stripe reads limited to batches of eight. This fails closed when payment verification is unavailable and prevents reordered notifications from restoring refunded credits. Verification work still grows with lifetime purchase history; test realistic account histories and provider rate limits before launch. A future durable reconciliation ledger must preserve refund, dispute and deletion correctness rather than trusting cached grants indefinitely.

Account deletion closes new purchases and model access durably, expires pending checkout, cancels recurring subscriptions and disables model keys before identity deletion. Cleanup failures remain retryable. Stripe notifications verify signatures and reconcile current payment state; duplicate or reordered notifications must not mint credits.

## Using credits in Vector

The desktop app and the CLI list **Vector Codium** (`vector-plan`) as a provider whenever the signed-in account's `/api/model-plans/models` answers `enabled: true`, which needs `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=true` and a complete server configuration. Every signed-in account then sees the catalog, with or without credits; the endpoint reads no billing state, so frequent catalog polls never reach Stripe or OpenRouter. The desktop app passes the account token from its main process to the local engine, and the CLI uses the `vct_` token from `vector login` or `VECTOR_CLI_TOKEN`. Neither the token nor repository configuration can change the provider's destination or models: requests go only to `/api/model-plans/chat`, with the token added at that final boundary. A catalog refresh that is slow, fails or returns something unusable keeps the account's last catalog, so a paid turn never fails on it; only a refused token or `enabled: false` removes Codium. A fresh engine starts from the last catalog it saved for the signed-in account (keyed by a digest of the token, never the token), and a turn on an account with no catalog at all waits for vectordev.ai's answer instead of giving up after the short listing deadline.

Both runtimes (the engine's AI SDK path and the Core V2 runner) stream text and tool calls through the gateway. The gateway forwards each reply's token usage without the provider's USD charge, and without the name of the provider that served it, its fingerprint or its own finish reason, and the catalog Vector receives carries no prices, because dollars spent beside the share of a plan used would reveal the plan's credit amount. The tokens themselves still narrow it: priced at public provider rates beside the share used, they let anyone estimate the credit amount, without revealing its stored value. A Codium step therefore records its tokens and counts as unpriced: session, Settings → Usage and CLI cost totals leave it out and note that some steps are not in the total. Codium is never chosen automatically, in the engine, the Core V2 runner, the app, the TUI, ACP editor sessions, Vectorscope reviews or agent swarms, even when it is the only provider listed, so credits are spent only on a model the person chose. The catalog's order is kept, and its first model is Codium's default within the provider. The app and the TUI read the provider list again whenever a model picker opens, so a catalog that arrived after the first listing is shown.

The gateway estimates a request's input tokens at four bytes of content per token, as Vector's clients do, and refuses one that cannot fit the model's listed context window with its reply (`MODEL_PLAN_CONTEXT`). Vector compacts a conversation once a step's provider-counted tokens reach the window less the reply, which comes first for text averaging up to four characters a token; for text with more characters a token the gateway's estimate reaches the window first, and its refusal makes Vector compact. Haiku requests also meet the prompt bound described under Coding catalog. A provider tokenizer can count more tokens than that estimate, so when the provider itself refuses a request's context (or answers 413), the gateway answers `MODEL_PLAN_CONTEXT` too, without passing on the provider's wording, and Vector compacts instead of retrying. The same estimate sizes the credit check that picks the monthly or purchased pool.

Settings → Account and Settings → Usage show the plan, the share of the month's credits left, whether purchased credits are available, running low or used up, and the renewal date from `/api/model-plans/status`, which accepts the same account token as chat requests (and the website's browser session). Like the account page, they show a share and states, never amounts: vectordev.ai works out the monthly share, rounded to whole percent, the purchased credits' state and both flags, so no amount reaches the app, the CLI or the browser. Purchased credits read as a state because they pool every top-up ever bought, so a share of them would mislead a repeat buyer; they are used up once they cannot cover the cheapest model's largest request, and running low under a tenth of the smallest top-up. **Buy credits** and **Manage billing and invoices** open Stripe in the desktop's billing window (see Account and credit flow); without the desktop bridge they open `/codium` and `/account`. A balance under a tenth of the month's plan credits, or of the smallest top-up, is flagged as running low; both thresholds scale with what was bought, so the share at which the warning appears reveals no credit amount, and purchased credits count every top-up ever bought, so they do not raise the threshold. A balance too small for the largest request of every Codium model, which the gateway would refuse, is flagged as used up. When that happens, including to a subscriber's monthly credits before they renew, Settings and `vector codium` say so and give the renewal date.

When the gateway answers that the account has no plan or not enough credits (402 with `MODEL_PLAN_REQUIRED`, `MODEL_PLAN_PAYMENT_REQUIRED`, `MODEL_PLAN_EXHAUSTED` or `MODEL_TOPUP_EXHAUSTED`), the turn fails at once with the gateway's explanation and a **Buy credits** action. A revoked or expired sign-in, or no sign-in at all, offers **Sign in to Vector**, and a disabled or misconfigured service, or a model no longer in the catalog, asks the person to choose another model. None of these answers is retried or sent to another provider. A failure Vector detects before sending (signed out, Codium off, model retired) reads like the gateway's answer and sends nothing. `MODEL_PLAN_CAPACITY` (402) means the provider refused the request for want of credit: Vector's model capacity, or the account key's own limit, which is the person's credit amount. It is retried like a busy provider and never asks for a purchase. Engine sessions show the action on the failed turn and on session notices; Core V2 sessions record it in the assistant error as `{ type: "codium", action }`. `vector codium` (alias `vector credits`) prints the plan and balances, and the purchase link only while Codium is on sale (signed out, it reads that from the public `/api/model-plans/config`); `vector models vector-plan` lists the models.

## Configuration and deferred activation

Server secrets belong in the hosting environment, never in client builds or the repository. See `.env.example` for the complete list:

- `MODEL_PLANS_ENABLED=false` while payment setup is deferred.
- `MODEL_CREDIT_MARKUP_PERCENT` sets the purchase-to-credit conversion. Set it privately; there is no fallback. If blank, explicit private credit amount overrides are required before purchases can become available.
- `MODEL_PLAN_<price>_CREDITS_USD` and `MODEL_TOPUP_<price>_CREDITS_USD` set private credit amounts; valid values are greater than zero and no greater than the purchase price.
- `MODEL_PLAN_MODELS_JSON` optionally overrides the catalog. Blank uses the reviewed defaults; `[]` intentionally offers no models.
- `STRIPE_MODEL_PLAN_<price>_PRICE_ID` and `STRIPE_MODEL_TOPUP_<price>_PRICE_ID` map each of the four prices ($10, $50, $100 and $200) to recurring and one-time prices respectively.
- Stripe secret, webhook secret and portal configuration; OpenRouter management key; a stable 32-byte AES encryption secret encoded as 64 hex characters; existing Vector/Supabase authentication and persistent Redis/Valkey REST configuration.

`bun scripts/setup-model-plans.ts` is a **dry run by default** and shows prices/credit amounts without connecting to Stripe. Its explicit `--apply` adapter can later create/reuse products, the cancellation portal and the `/api/model-plans/webhook` endpoint (API version `2025-02-24.acacia`, with only the events in `MODEL_PLAN_WEBHOOK_EVENTS`) in the intended test account. Do not run `--apply` until the owner resumes Stripe setup. Keep sales disabled until the full test flow is verified.

## Payment automation

Once configured, no payment needs a person. The webhook grants credits as soon as Stripe confirms a payment: a capped key for the paid month, renewed on a fresh key each period, and a purchased-credit key that each top-up raises. Failed renewals, cancellations, refunds and disputes take credits away. OpenRouter cannot be paid through an API, so Vector's OpenRouter balance is refilled by OpenRouter's own auto top-up. A 10-minute cron (`/api/model-plans/funding`) compares that balance with what every Codium key can still spend, plus the credits that open checkouts reserve and room for the largest purchase. Each checkout reserves its credits atomically when it opens, so purchases between checks cannot together oversell the balance. When the balance falls short of that plus a margin, or a check cannot read OpenRouter, it pauses new checkouts and top-ups at once and emails the owner; a sale whose newest reading is older than 15 minutes runs a check first. Sales reopen on their own at the first check that succeeds and finds the balance covers it. Each Codium charge also writes a private ledger entry: price, refunds, Stripe's fee from the charge's balance transaction, the credits granted, OpenRouter's fee estimate and the profit. The owner reads it at `/api/model-plans/ledger` with `CODIUM_OWNER_SECRET`; everyone else gets a 404. The owner's setup list is [owner-actions/codium-payments.md](owner-actions/codium-payments.md).

OpenRouter's [terms, section 7](https://openrouter.ai/terms) restrict resale of model API access. Confirm that the bundled Vector offering is covered by appropriate commercial terms before activation. No source change grants this permission.

Remaining launch work requires owner authentication/business verification, funded capacity, actual payment/provider fee terms, secret configuration, legal review of the Codium terms, payment activation, and end-to-end test payments, renewal, cancellation, refund and coding-task checks. Publishing the coming-soon website does not enable paid access. No live Stripe connection, real purchase or provider funding was performed as part of this implementation.
